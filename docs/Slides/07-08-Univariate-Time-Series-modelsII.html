1  The autoregressive process AR(1)

AR(1) Perocess

\[ Y_t=\phi Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t, \qquad \varepsilon_t\sim WN(0,\sigma^2). \]

Substituting the recursion into itself gives the recursive (backward) solution

\[ Y_t=\phi^t Y_0+\sum_{i=0}^{t-1}\phi^{\,i}\varepsilon_{t-i}, \]

and projecting forward \(j\) periods gives the forward solution

\[ Y_{t+j}=\phi^{\,j+1}Y_{t-1}+\sum_{i=0}^{j}\phi^{\,i}\varepsilon_{t+j-i},\qquad j>0. \]

The forward form is the natural starting point for forecasting; the backward form shows the initial condition \(Y_0\) fading (or growing) as \(\phi^{\,t}\).

Dynamic multipliers and the impulse response

Because \(Y_t\) is a weighted sum of past shocks, we can ask exactly how much a single innovation matters, and for how long.

The dynamic multiplier is

\[ \frac{\partial Y_t}{\partial \varepsilon_{t-j}}=\phi^{\,j} \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad \frac{\partial Y_{t+j}}{\partial \varepsilon_t}=\phi^{\,j}. \]

Plotted against the horizon \(j\), the sequence \(\{\phi^{\,j}\}\) is the impulse response function (IRF)

Dynamic multipliers and the impulse response

The cumulative effect is \(\sum_{i=0}^{j}\phi^{\,i}\), and for \(|\phi|<1\) the long-run (total) effect of a unit shock is

\[ \lim_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{j}\phi^{\,i}=\frac{1}{1-\phi}. \]

The closer \(\phi\) is to one, the more slowly the response decays and the larger the accumulated effect — this is exactly what we mean by persistence.

Stability, stationarity, and the Wold form

Three cases exhaust the AR(1):

condition behaviour
\(\lvert\phi\rvert<1\) stationary; shocks decay geometrically
\(\phi=1\) random walk; a unit root, non-stationary
\(\phi=-1\) alternating unit root, non-stationary
\(\lvert\phi\rvert>1\) explosive

When \(|\phi|<1\) the backward solution converges (\(\phi^tY_0\) vanishes) and we recover the Wold / MA(\(\infty\)) representation

\[ Y_t=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi^{\,j}\varepsilon_{t-j}. \]

Drift, moments, and the half-life

Adding a constant, for \(|\phi|<1\),

\[ Y_t=c+\phi Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Y_t=\frac{c}{1-\phi}+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}\phi^{\,i}\varepsilon_{t-i}. \]

The stationary moments are

\[ \mu=\frac{c}{1-\phi}, \qquad \gamma_0=\frac{\sigma^2}{1-\phi^2}, \qquad \gamma_j=\phi^{\,j}\gamma_0, \qquad \rho_j=\phi^{\,j}. \]

Autocorrelation Function

The ACF decays geometrically rather than cutting off — the defining contrast with an MA process. A convenient summary of persistence is the half-life:

\[ \phi^{\,h}=\tfrac12 \quad\Longrightarrow\quad h=\frac{\ln 0.5}{\ln\phi}\qquad(0<\phi<1). \]

MA: ACF cuts off, PACF decays. AR(1): ACF decays, PACF cuts off after lag 1. The two are mirror images — the seed of Box–Jenkins identification.

The lag operator and the characteristic root

The lag operator writes the AR(1) compactly, and turns stationarity into a statement about a root:

\[ (1-\phi L)\,Y_t=\varepsilon_t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad \text{characteristic polynomial }1-\phi z, \quad z^\star=\frac{1}{\phi}. \]

Stationarity requires the root outside the unit circle, \(|z^\star|>1\), which is exactly \(|\phi|<1\).

The lag operator and the characteristic root

Inverting the operator as a geometric series reproduces the Wold form:

\[ (1-\phi L)^{-1}=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi^{\,j}L^{\,j} \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Y_t=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\phi^{\,j}\varepsilon_{t-j}=\Psi(L)\varepsilon_t. \]

“Root outside the unit circle” and “\(|\phi|<1\)” are the same condition. The root language is what generalises cleanly to AR(2) and AR(\(p\)).

\[ \phi=0.8\;(c=0.4), \qquad \phi=0.4\;(c=1.2), \qquad \phi=-0.7\;(c=3.4). \]

  • \(\phi=0.8\) is most persistent: it wanders for long stretches; its ACF \(\rho_j=0.8^{\,j}\) decays slowly.

2  The AR(2) process

Moments: the Yule–Walker equations

\[ Y_t=c+\phi_1 Y_{t-1}+\phi_2 Y_{t-2}+\varepsilon_t, \qquad \mu=\frac{c}{1-\phi_1-\phi_2}. \]

Multiplying the mean-corrected equation by \(Y_{t-j}\) and taking expectations gives the Yule–Walker recursion:

\[ \gamma_0=\phi_1\gamma_1+\phi_2\gamma_2+\sigma^2, \qquad \gamma_j=\phi_1\gamma_{j-1}+\phi_2\gamma_{j-2}\quad(j\ge1), \]

and, dividing by \(\gamma_0\),

\[ \rho_j=\phi_1\rho_{j-1}+\phi_2\rho_{j-2}\qquad(j\ge1). \]

From \(\rho_1,\rho_2\) onward the ACF is generated by the same second-order recursion, so it decays (possibly with oscillations) rather than cutting off.

The AR(2) autocorrelations

Solving the first two Yule–Walker equations explicitly is worth memorising:

\[ \boxed{\;\rho_1=\frac{\phi_1}{1-\phi_2}, \qquad \rho_2=\phi_2+\frac{\phi_1^2}{1-\phi_2}=\phi_2+\phi_1\rho_1,\;} \]

with variance

\[ \gamma_0=\frac{(1-\phi_2)\,\sigma^2}{(1+\phi_2)\big[(1-\phi_2)^2-\phi_1^2\big]}. \]

Stationarity: roots, the triangle, and complex roots

In lag-operator form \((1-\phi_1 L-\phi_2 L^2)\,Y_t=c+\varepsilon_t\), with characteristic equation

\[ 1-\phi_1 z-\phi_2 z^2=0, \qquad z_{1,2}=\frac{-\phi_1\pm\sqrt{\phi_1^2+4\phi_2}}{2\phi_2}. \]

The process is stationary when both roots lie outside the unit circle — equivalently, inside the stationarity triangle

\[ \phi_1+\phi_2<1, \qquad \phi_2-\phi_1<1, \qquad -1<\phi_2<1. \]

Stationarity: roots, the triangle, and complex roots

The discriminant \(\phi_1^2+4\phi_2\) decides the dynamics:

  • real distinct roots (\(>0\)): ACF/IRF is a sum of two decaying geometrics — monotone or sign-alternating decay;
  • complex-conjugate roots (\(<0\)): a damped sinusoid — pseudo-cycles of modulus \(r=\sqrt{-\phi_2}\) and

\[ \text{Period}=\frac{2\pi}{\arccos\!\big(\phi_1/(2\sqrt{-\phi_2})\big)}. \]

Complex roots are why the AR(2) is the simplest device for capturing business-cycle-like behaviour — cycles with no deterministic clock.

3  The general AR(\(p\)) process

Definition and the stationarity condition

\[ Y_t=c+\phi_1 Y_{t-1}+\cdots+\phi_p Y_{t-p}+\varepsilon_t \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad \phi(L)\,Y_t=c+\varepsilon_t, \]

with AR lag polynomial

\[ \phi(z)=1-\phi_1 z-\phi_2 z^2-\cdots-\phi_p z^p. \]

An AR(\(p\)) is stable and covariance-stationary iff all \(p\) roots of \(\phi(z)=0\) have modulus greater than one — i.e. all lie outside the unit circle.

Moments and the Wold form

The Yule–Walker relations extend directly:

\[ \mu=\frac{c}{1-\phi_1-\cdots-\phi_p}, \qquad \rho_j=\phi_1\rho_{j-1}+\cdots+\phi_p\rho_{j-p}\quad(j>0), \]

\[ \gamma_j= \begin{cases} \phi_1\gamma_1+\cdots+\phi_p\gamma_p+\sigma^2, & j=0,\\[4pt] \phi_1\gamma_{j-1}+\cdots+\phi_p\gamma_{j-p}, & j>0. \end{cases} \]

Factorising \(\phi(L)=\prod_{i=1}^{p}(1-\lambda_i L)\) and inverting each factor (valid when every \(|\lambda_i|<1\)) gives the Wold / MA(\(\infty\)) form for \(X_t=Y_t-\mu\):

\[ X_t=\psi(L)\varepsilon_t, \qquad \psi(L)=\prod_{i=1}^{p}\Big(\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}\lambda_i^{\,j}L^{\,j}\Big), \]

whose coefficients \(\psi_j\) are the impulse response.

Companion form and the eigenvalue condition

Checking \(p\) polynomial roots by hand is awkward. The companion form turns the AR(\(p\)) into a first-order vector recursion whose stability is an eigenvalue problem:

\[ \mathbf Z_t=F\,\mathbf Z_{t-1}+v_t, \quad \mathbf Z_t=\begin{bmatrix}X_t\\ X_{t-1}\\ \vdots\\ X_{t-p+1}\end{bmatrix}, \quad F=\begin{bmatrix} \phi_1 & \phi_2 & \cdots & \phi_{p-1} & \phi_p\\ 1 & 0 & \cdots & 0 & 0\\ 0 & 1 & \cdots & 0 & 0\\ \vdots & & \ddots & & \vdots\\ 0 & 0 & \cdots & 1 & 0 \end{bmatrix}. \]

Stationary iff all eigenvalues of \(F\) lie inside the unit circle, \(|\lambda_i|<1\). The eigenvalues of \(F\) are exactly the reciprocals of the characteristic roots — “eigenvalues inside” and “roots outside” say the same thing.

R — two equivalent stationarity checks

# Two equivalent stationarity checks for AR(2) model A: (0.5, 0.25)
ar_A <- c(0.5, 0.25)

# (i) roots of 1 - phi1 z - phi2 z^2 : want modulus > 1
roots_A <- polyroot(c(1, -ar_A[1], -ar_A[2]))
cat("Characteristic roots:", round(roots_A, 3), " | moduli:",
    round(Mod(roots_A), 3), "(outside unit circle)\n")

# (ii) eigenvalues of the companion matrix F : want modulus < 1
F_A <- rbind(ar_A, c(1, 0))
eig_A <- eigen(F_A)$values
cat("Companion eigenvalues:", round(eig_A, 3), " | moduli:",
    round(Mod(eig_A), 3), "(inside unit circle)\n")
cat("Reciprocal check 1/roots:", round(1/roots_A, 3), "\n")

roots_plot(roots_A, title = "AR(2) model A: characteristic roots vs unit circle")

The two checks are numerically dual: the companion eigenvalues equal \(1/\text{roots}\). If either test puts a value on the wrong side of the unit circle, the process is not stationary.

4  The partial autocorrelation function

What the PACF measures

For an AR(\(p\)) the ACF decays and never cleanly signals the order \(p\). The PACF fixes this. The \(j\)-th partial autocorrelation is the correlation between \(Y_t\) and \(Y_{t-j}\) after removing the linear influence of the intervening lags:

\[ \phi_{jj}=\operatorname{Corr}\big(Y_t,\,Y_{t-j}\mid Y_{t-1},\ldots,Y_{t-j+1}\big). \]

Operationally, on mean-corrected data \(X_t=Y_t-\mu\), run the nested regressions

\[ X_t=\phi_{11}X_{t-1}+e_t, \] \[ X_t=\phi_{12}X_{t-1}+\phi_{22}X_{t-2}+e_t, \] \[ X_t=\phi_{13}X_{t-1}+\phi_{23}X_{t-2}+\phi_{33}X_{t-3}+e_t,\ \ldots \]

The last coefficient \(\phi_{jj}\) is the \(j\)-th PACF value: the extra explanatory power of \(Y_{t-j}\) once all shorter lags are in.

The identification duality

The identification duality. For an AR(\(p\)) the PACF cuts off after lag \(p\) while the ACF decays. For an MA(\(q\)) it is the mirror image: the ACF cuts off after lag \(q\) while the PACF decays. Reading the two together is the heart of Box–Jenkins identification.

The ACF answers “how far back does correlation reach?”; the PACF answers “how far back does direct dependence reach?” AR order lives in the second question.

R — ACF decays, PACF cuts off at the AR order

5  Invertibility: the dual of stationarity

Invertibility of an MA process

Just as a stationary AR can be written as an MA(\(\infty\)), an invertible MA can be written as an AR(\(\infty\)). Invertibility makes the innovation recoverable from current and past observations — indispensable for forecasting and for a unique parameterisation (recall the MA(1) \(\theta\) vs \(1/\theta\) ambiguity).

For an MA(1),

\[ Y_t-\mu=(1+\beta L)\varepsilon_t \;\Longrightarrow\; \varepsilon_t=(1+\beta L)^{-1}(Y_t-\mu)=\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}(-\beta)^{\,j}(Y_{t-j}-\mu), \]

which converges iff \(|\beta|<1\): the root of \(1+\beta z=0\) lies outside the unit circle. In general MA(\(q\)) is invertible iff every root of \(1+\beta_1 z+\cdots+\beta_q z^q=0\) lies outside the unit circle.

The exact mirror of AR stationarity: stationarity constrains the AR roots, invertibility constrains the MA roots. Same “outside the unit circle” rule, different polynomial.

R — checking MA(2) invertibility

# Invertibility of an MA(2): Y_t = eps_t + 1.0 eps_{t-1} + 0.7 eps_{t-2}
ma_roots <- polyroot(c(1, 1.0, 0.7))     # 1 + 1.0 z + 0.7 z^2
cat("MA(2) roots:", round(ma_roots, 3), "\n")
cat("moduli     :", round(Mod(ma_roots), 3), " -> all > 1, so invertible\n")

Both roots have modulus \(\approx 1.20>1\), so this MA(2) is invertible and has a well-defined AR(\(\infty\)) representation.

Predict: two invertible MA(2) processes

\[ Y_{1t}=2+\varepsilon_t+1.0\,\varepsilon_{t-1}+0.7\,\varepsilon_{t-2}, \qquad Y_{2t}=2+\varepsilon_t-1.2\,\varepsilon_{t-1}+0.25\,\varepsilon_{t-2}. \]

Predict: the ACF should cut off after lag 2 for both. The PACF should decay (monotone or oscillatory, depending on the MA roots) rather than cut off — the reverse of the AR pattern.

R — two MA(2) processes

# Two MA(2) processes: realizations, ACF (cuts off at 2), PACF (decays)
set.seed(123)
n <- 250
ma_1 <- c(1.0, 0.7)
ma_2 <- c(-1.2, 0.25)

s1 <- 2 + arima.sim(model = list(ma = ma_1), n = n)
s2 <- 2 + arima.sim(model = list(ma = ma_2), n = n)

grid.arrange(
  ts_plot(s1, mu = 2, title = "MA(2): theta = (1.0, 0.7)"),
  ts_plot(s2, mu = 2, title = "MA(2): theta = (-1.2, 0.25)"),
  acf_plot(ma = ma_1, title = "ACF: cuts off after lag 2"),
  acf_plot(ma = ma_2, title = "ACF: cuts off after lag 2"),
  pacf_plot(ma = ma_1, title = "PACF: decays"),
  pacf_plot(ma = ma_2, title = "PACF: decays"),
  ncol = 2
)

6  The ARMA(\(p,q\)) process

Mixing the two families

\[ Y_t=c+\phi_1 Y_{t-1}+\cdots+\phi_p Y_{t-p} +\varepsilon_t+\beta_1\varepsilon_{t-1}+\cdots+\beta_q\varepsilon_{t-q}, \]

or, with \(\phi(L)=1-\phi_1 L-\cdots-\phi_p L^p\) and \(\beta(L)=1+\beta_1 L+\cdots+\beta_q L^q\),

\[ \phi(L)\,Y_t=c+\beta(L)\,\varepsilon_t. \]

Two separate conditions govern its behaviour:

  • Stationarity depends only on the AR polynomial \(\phi(L)\) — its roots outside the unit circle.
  • Invertibility depends only on the MA polynomial \(\beta(L)\) — its roots outside the unit circle.

When stationary, the Wold representation is \(Y_t=\phi(L)^{-1}c+\dfrac{\beta(L)}{\phi(L)}\varepsilon_t\).

Moments and the ARMA(1,1)

Past lag \(q\), the ACF obeys the pure-AR recursion; for \(j\le q\) the MA terms add extra covariance. For the ARMA(1,1) \(Y_t=c+\phi Y_{t-1}+\varepsilon_t+\beta\varepsilon_{t-1}\),

\[ \mu=\frac{c}{1-\phi}, \qquad \gamma_0=\frac{1+2\phi\beta+\beta^2}{1-\phi^2}\,\sigma^2, \qquad \gamma_1=\frac{(1+\phi\beta)(\phi+\beta)}{1-\phi^2}\,\sigma^2, \]

\[ \rho_1=\frac{(1+\phi\beta)(\phi+\beta)}{1+2\phi\beta+\beta^2}, \qquad \rho_j=\phi\,\rho_{j-1}\ \ (j\ge2). \]

The ACF decays geometrically at rate \(\phi\) after lag 1, but the first-lag value blends both the AR and MA parameters.

Parameter redundancy (common factors)

ARMA models are identified only if \(\phi(L)\) and \(\beta(L)\) share no common root. If they do, the common factor cancels and the model is over-parameterised:

\[ (1-0.5L)\,Y_t=(1-0.5L)\,\varepsilon_t \quad\Longrightarrow\quad Y_t=\varepsilon_t, \]

an “ARMA(1,1)” that is really white noise.

Before trusting an estimated ARMA(\(p,q\)), check the AR and MA roots are not (nearly) cancelling. Near-common factors show up as unstable estimates — a standard reason to prefer a smaller, parsimonious model.

Predict + R — an ARMA(2,2)

\[ Y_t=-0.6Y_{t-1}+0.2Y_{t-2} +\varepsilon_t-1.2\varepsilon_{t-1}+0.25\varepsilon_{t-2}. \]

AR part reuses model D (stationary); MA part reuses the second MA(2) (invertible).

Predict: with \(p>0\) and \(q>0\), neither the ACF nor the PACF should cut off cleanly — both decay.

# ARMA(2,2): stationary AR part (D) + invertible MA part
set.seed(123)
ar_arma <- c(-0.6, 0.2)
ma_arma <- c(-1.2, 0.25)

y_arma <- arima.sim(model = list(ar = ar_arma, ma = ma_arma), n = 250)

grid.arrange(
  ts_plot(y_arma, title = "ARMA(2,2) realization"),
  acf_plot(ar = ar_arma, ma = ma_arma, title = "ACF: decays (no clean cut-off)"),
  pacf_plot(ar = ar_arma, ma = ma_arma, title = "PACF: decays (no clean cut-off)"),
  ncol = 1
)

7  Identifying a model from ACF and PACF

The identification table

The ACF and PACF together fingerprint the model class. This is the practical payoff of the whole lecture.

Process ACF PACF
White noise zero at all lags \(>0\) zero at all lags \(>0\)
AR(\(p\)) decays (monotone or oscillatory) cuts off after lag \(p\)
MA(\(q\)) cuts off after lag \(q\) decays (monotone or oscillatory)
ARMA(\(p,q\)) decays after lag \(q\) decays after lag \(p\)

With real data these patterns are seen through sampling noise, so identification narrows the field to a few candidate \((p,q)\) — the final choice is settled by estimation and diagnostics next lecture.

Sign patterns worth remembering

  • AR(1), \(\phi>0\): ACF is positive geometric decay; \(\phi<0\): ACF alternates sign. PACF is a single spike \(\phi_{11}=\phi\).
  • MA(1), \(\beta>0\): single positive ACF spike at lag 1; \(\beta<0\): single negative spike. PACF decays.
  • ARMA(1,1): geometric ACF decay after lag 1 with \(\operatorname{sign}(\rho_1)=\operatorname{sign}(\phi+\beta)\); PACF decays after lag 1.

Recap

Key takeaways

  • AR(1): read persistence off the IRF \(\psi_j=\phi^{\,j}\), the half-life \(\ln 0.5/\ln\phi\), and the long-run effect \(1/(1-\phi)\).
  • Stationarity = characteristic roots outside the unit circle; recover the Wold / MA(\(\infty\)) form by inverting \(\phi(L)\).
  • AR(2): Yule–Walker moments, the stationarity triangle, and complex roots giving damped cycles of period \(2\pi/\arccos\!\big(\phi_1/2\sqrt{-\phi_2}\big)\).
  • AR(\(p\)): companion form \(F\); stationarity iff all eigenvalues inside the unit circle (= reciprocals of the roots).
  • PACF and the duality: AR \(\Rightarrow\) PACF cuts off; MA \(\Rightarrow\) ACF cuts off.
  • Invertibility is the MA-side mirror of stationarity (MA roots outside the unit circle).
  • ARMA(\(p,q\)): stationarity from \(\phi(L)\), invertibility from \(\beta(L)\); watch for common-factor redundancy.
